# Discovery

## Discover first

Read what is available before asking: docs, repo, PR, ticket, notes. Establish:

- The feature and its purpose.
- How the work was done before, and the pain in it.
- What changed and how it works now.
- Who benefits.
- If you can trace the code, check whether the document or ticket drifts from the end code. Merged code is always the only source of truth.

## Ask for what only the presenter knows

Documents don't hold everything. Some of what makes a good script is only in the presenter's head. After reading, ask for whatever is still missing from this list, in one short message of at most four conversational questions. Skip anything you already found.

- **Links to show.** Which pages, repos, dashboards or files will be on screen, and at which moment? Ask for the URL of each one that matters.
- **Decisions and who was involved.** Did you ask anyone before building, or choose between options? Who, and why did you pick this one?
- **Where it stands today.** What is done, what is not, and what will the next demo cover?
- **Corrections.** Does any number or claim in the docs look out of date? What did you actually see?
- **Audience.** Who is listening, and what should they leave knowing or doing?

Do not send a questionnaire. Do not ask what the documents already answer.

## Find the business reason

Look for the real outcome behind the change: time saved, fewer manual steps, fewer errors, faster turnaround, lower cost, better customer experience, easier scaling, better access or collaboration. Pick the one or two that are true here. Do not list them all.
